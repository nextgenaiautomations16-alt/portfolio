The problem

Before assigning a load to a carrier, a broker needs to confirm three things: the carrier's operating authority is active, their insurance is current and sufficient, and their FMCSA safety rating is acceptable. Skipping or rushing this check is how brokers end up liable when something goes wrong with a carrier that shouldn't have been hauling in the first place.

This isn't a hypothetical risk — brokerage tech vendors build entire product lines around automating carrier vetting, which is a reasonable signal that manual vetting is a real, expensive bottleneck in the industry today.

Evidence this is a real, active problem

  • Carrier vetting is a named feature, not an afterthought. AI-native TMS challengers market automated carrier onboarding and vetting as a core selling point — proof brokers are actively paying to solve this.
  • The regulatory data already exists publicly. FMCSA's SAFER system tracks operating authority, safety rating, and insurance status for every registered carrier — the bottleneck isn't data availability, it's someone manually checking it every time.
  • It pairs directly with a hiring-relevant skill set. Forward Deployed Engineer postings in freight/logistics explicitly list integrating with carrier and safety data sources as a core responsibility.

How it works

A new carrier's details come in, get checked against FMCSA's public safety and authority data, and run through a deterministic rules engine. Carriers with a genuine regulatory disqualifier are rejected automatically — no one needs to weigh in. Carriers with a real judgment call (new to the industry, insurance slightly under the minimum) get routed to a human with the specific reasons attached.

Deliberate design decisions

Hard rejects don't page a human

A carrier with revoked authority or no insurance on file isn't a judgment call — the regulation already answered the question. Alerting a human on every flagged carrier, rejects included, would just be noise. The system earns its keep specifically in the "review" bucket, where a real decision actually has to be made.

The vetting rules are explicit, not model-inferred

Same principle as LoadAudit AI: the LLM/lookup layer gathers information, but the approve/review/reject decision comes from a rules engine a broker can audit line by line — important when a carrier disputes a rejection.

Real vs. simulated is stated plainly

The FMCSA integration has a real API code path (for a free FMCSA web key) and a local fallback for demo purposes — both are documented as exactly what they are in the README, rather than presenting a demo as more "live" than it is.

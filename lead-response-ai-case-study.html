The problem

Real estate leads come in from Zillow, a website form, a Facebook ad, an open house sign-in — often at odd hours, often in a flood. Every one of them needs a fast reply, but only some of them are actually ready to buy right now. Most teams can't staff someone to watch every channel around the clock, so a genuinely hot lead — someone pre-approved and ready to move — can sit in the same queue as someone just browsing, and lose the window where a fast call would have mattered.

Why speed is the whole point here

  • Response time has an outsized effect on conversion. The gap between calling a lead back in minutes versus hours is one of the most consistently cited factors in real estate lead conversion — this isn't a marginal optimization, it's often the difference between a lead converting at all.
  • Not every lead deserves the same urgency. Treating every inbound message as equally time-sensitive burns out an agent's attention on browsers and vague inquiries, leaving less energy for the leads that are actually ready.
  • This connects directly to real work I've done. I've built real-estate-specific lead-follow-up positioning before through NextGen AI Automations — this project is that same instinct, built out as a working system with real evaluation behind it.

How it works

A lead comes in, gets scored against a small set of explicit qualifiers, and gets an instant reply no matter what — the one thing that never happens is a lead getting no response at all.

Deliberate design decisions

Every lead gets a reply, regardless of tier

It would be easy to only bother drafting a response for hot leads and let warm/cold ones sit. That's exactly backwards — the leads most likely to feel ignored (and go find another agent) are the ones who aren't obviously urgent yet. The instant reply is universal; only the human alert is tiered.

The eval checks that hot scoring and hot replies never disagree

It's not enough for the tier classification to be accurate — a "hot" lead that somehow ends up with no drafted reply attached has failed at the actual job. The evaluation harness checks this as its own separate condition, not just folded into overall tier accuracy.

The Slack alert is designed to be actionable, not just informative

It doesn't just say "new hot lead" — it states the qualifying reasons and an explicit "call within 5 minutes" framing, because the entire value of this system lives in how fast a human acts on it, not in how sophisticated the scoring math is.

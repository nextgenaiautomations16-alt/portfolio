The problem

Before a broker pays a carrier invoice, someone has to check it against three things: the rate confirmation, the load tender, and any pre-authorized accessorial charges (detention, layover, lumper fees). This "3-way match" is manual, slow, and error-prone — done right, it catches real overbilling; done under time pressure, it doesn't.

A mid-size shipper running 500 loads a month spends real hours a week on this. Industry benchmarks put 5–10% of invoices at having at least one billing discrepancy — money that gets paid out unless someone catches it first.

Evidence this is a real, active problem

  • Manual audit is expensive. Automated invoice reconciliation is reported to cut discrepancy-resolution time 65–75% versus fully manual review, and typically recovers 3–8% of total audited freight spend from caught overbilling.
  • Vendors are building entire products around this. Freight-audit and AI-native TMS companies market this exact workflow as a core feature — proof brokers are already paying to solve it.
  • Forward Deployed Engineer roles in freight/logistics are actively hiring right now — including postings that explicitly list TMS integrations and BOL/rate-confirmation knowledge as requirements.

How it works

An invoice comes in, gets parsed into structured data, and gets checked against the rate confirmation on file. Clean matches clear automatically. Anything that fails a check gets a plain-language explanation, a drafted dispute email, and a real-time alert to a human — instead of quietly getting approved.

Deliberate design decisions

The match decision is rule-based, not LLM-judged

The LLM's job is turning messy invoice text into structured data. A deterministic engine makes the actual approve/flag call, because that decision needs to hold up to an AP manager or auditor after the fact — "the model felt it looked fine" isn't a defensible reason to release payment.

It's evaluated honestly, not demoed once and called done

A labeled synthetic dataset and an evaluation harness score precision and recall on every run — python -m eval.run_eval — rather than relying on one good-looking example.

The human stays in the loop

Flagged invoices don't get silently rejected or silently approved — they land in Slack with the reason and a drafted response, so a person makes the final call with full context in seconds, not minutes of digging.
